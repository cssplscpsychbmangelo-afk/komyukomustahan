01 Before you start

This activity asks people to name what they wish they could change — in their own lives, and in the world. That is a real question, and some participants will answer it honestly about hard things. Set the tone before you begin: no one has to explain an answer, and no one is diagnosed by a card.

What you need

One device per person (ideal) Or one shared screen Or printed cards A timer

Nothing needs installing or signing up for. The activity is a single web page that runs entirely offline — no answers are sent anywhere, and reflections are saved only in the participant's own browser. If people are on phones, they can simply open the link.

One shared screen? Run the page on the projector and let the room answer by show of hands for each question — count the majority per question (A/B/C/D), then reveal the power the room chose together. It lands well as a group verdict.

The whole thing fits in a quarter of an hour. Here is where the time goes.

  • 0:07

  • 0:09

  • 0:12

  • 0:14

03 The opening spiel

Read this as written the first time. It is paced for speaking aloud.

Opening

Imagine waking up tomorrow with a superpower.

Not just any superpower, but one that reflects what you need, what you value, and what you wish you could change in the world today.

Maybe you want to protect someone. Maybe you want to escape from everything for a while. Maybe you want to fight injustice, heal people, or simply have enough time to breathe.

In this activity, you will answer three simple questions. Your answers will be combined to reveal a superpower that you will carry for the rest of this activity.

Once you discover your power, think about this: if you had this power today, where would you use it, who would you use it for, and what would you change?

There are no wrong answers. Your power is yours to define.

05 The reveal formula

The superpower is not decided by one answer. It is decided by the combination — which is why changing even one answer changes the card a participant receives.

Assign each answer a value: A = 0, B = 1, C = 2, D = 3. Then:

Power Index = 1 + ((5·Q1 + 3·Q2 + 7·Q3) mod 16)

The result is a number from 1 to 16, and each number is one of the powers below. That gives 64 possible answer combinations mapping onto 16 powers. The weights (5, 3, 7) mean every question matters, and every question can flip the outcome.

Why the odds aren't even. Some powers sit on five combinations, others on three. That is a natural result of the modulo — not a bias you need to fix. If a participant asks, it is a good excuse to say: the point is not the arithmetic, it is what you do with what you got.

The full mapping

Power index → power → combinations that reach it
IndexPowerCombinationsCount

A combination is written Q1Q2Q3 — for example BCD means Q1 = B, Q2 = C, Q3 = D.

06 Dealing the cards

Don't simply announce the powers. Make the reveal an experience.

Before the reveal

Your answers have been processed. Your choices have revealed something about the power you carry. But remember, a superpower is only as meaningful as the way you choose to use it.

Then let them see their card. Give the room ten seconds of quiet to actually read it before anyone comments. When you do speak, ask the small questions first:

Did the card surprise you? · Would you have chosen this power yourself? · Which part of the description sounds most like you?

07 The reflection

Give one to two minutes of quiet writing. The page has a timer built in.

My superpower, my purpose

1. My superpower is __________.

2. I would use it in __________.

3. I need this power because __________.

4. If I could change one thing using this power, it would be __________.

Encourage participants to connect their answers to two dimensions:

  • Personal life. Family, friendships, studies, personal struggles, mental space, and aspirations.
  • The world we live in. Poverty, inequality, discrimination, lack of opportunities, suppression of voices, injustice, and other forms of deprivation.
Sharing is opt-in. Ask for volunteers, never names in sequence. Invite people to read only the line they are comfortable reading, and to pass without explaining.

08 The final twist

End the activity with this question, and give it real air:

The turn

After discovering your superpower, what is one thing you can already do today without having that power?

This matters because the activity should not end with an imaginary ability. It should help people recognise that some of the things they wish they could do are already within their capacity — even if they cannot solve everything alone.

If the room is quiet, seed it with scale: a message, a boundary, a question asked, a person checked on, a space made safer, a small thing refused.

09 The closing line

Read it once, slowly, and then stop.

Closing

Maybe we cannot stop time, heal every wound, rewrite reality, or protect everyone from everything. But we can choose to listen, speak up, help someone, stand with others, and make things a little better.

Sometimes, the world doesn't need another superhero. It needs ordinary people who refuse to be indifferent.

10 Holding the room

A kumustahan asks how are you — and sometimes the honest answer is heavy. A few things that keep the activity safe:

  • Cards are not diagnoses. Say it out loud. A power describes what someone reaches for, not who they are.
  • No forced disclosure. Writing is private and stays on the device; sharing is a choice.
  • Watch the quiet ones. Someone choosing “find peace and escape” twice may be telling you something. Follow up privately afterwards, not in front of the room.
  • Don't rescue too fast. If someone shares something hard, thank them before you reframe it.
  • Know your referral. If your context has a counsellor or guidance office, mention it at the start so the door is visibly open all the way through.

11 Print & offline

The activity works with no internet at all — it's a static page with no external requests. If you need a paper version, this guide prints cleanly (the reveal table and the scripts survive the printer), and the deck on the 16 powers can be printed card by card.

For a fully offline session, save this folder to a laptop or a USB stick and open index.html in any browser. Participants' answers stay on whichever device they used. The published copy lives at cssplscpsychbmangelo-afk.github.io/komyukomustahan.
